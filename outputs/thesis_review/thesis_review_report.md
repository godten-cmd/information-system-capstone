## II. Issue Register — Top 20 Issues Before Submission

### CRITICAL Issues (must fix before defense)

---

**Issue 1**
- **Severity**: Critical
- **Chapter**: Methodology (Ch. 3), Discussion (Ch. 5)
- **Description**: **Oracle label circularity**. The oracle strategy labels were assigned using the same heuristic mapping (temporal→BM25, exception→Dense, multi\_hop→Hybrid) that was subsequently encoded verbatim in the rule planner's rules R01–R04. This means the rule planner's SSA of 78.4% is evaluated against a ground truth that was co-designed with the rule planner's logic. This is not disclosed clearly in the methodology and only partially acknowledged in the Discussion. A reader examining SSA results without understanding this circularity would reach incorrect conclusions about the rule planner's generalizability.
- **Recommendation**: Add an explicit subsection in Methodology titled "Oracle Label Construction and Circularity" that acknowledges this limitation. In the Discussion and Threats to Validity, quantify the impact: 4/7 rules (R01–R04) directly encode oracle logic. Reframe the Rule Planner's SSA as "oracle-aligned accuracy" rather than a general planning quality measure. Consider constructing an independent oracle from retrieval experiments (the strategy achieving highest Recall@10 per query) and reporting SSA against both.

---

**Issue 2**
- **Severity**: Critical
- **Chapter**: Results (Ch. 4), Conclusion (Ch. 6)
- **Description**: **No statistical significance testing**. The thesis reports performance differences as small as 0.5–1.1 pp between the top systems (Rule Planner Recall@10=0.755 vs. GPT-5=0.750; Hybrid MRR=0.530 vs. Rule=0.522) and draws causal conclusions ("Rule Planner outperforms Hybrid") without any significance test. With n=380 queries and binary outcome Recall@1 (Hit/Miss), bootstrap confidence intervals or a paired permutation test would require only a few lines of Python. Differences of 0.5 pp are almost certainly not statistically significant.
- **Recommendation**: Run bootstrap confidence intervals (B=10,000) for primary metrics (Recall@10, MRR) for all five systems. Report 95% CI in the main results table. Where CIs overlap, use cautious language ("non-significantly better"). Add a statistical analysis paragraph to Section 4.6. Note: this does not require rerunning experiments, only post-hoc statistical analysis of existing result files.

**Issue 3**
- **Severity**: Critical
- **Chapter**: Results (Ch. 4, Section 4.3.2)
- **Description**: **Temporal query pool n=5 is too small for meaningful conclusions**. The thesis states "Dense achieves zero recall on temporal queries" and "GPT-5 achieves 0% SSA on temporal queries" based on five queries — one in six hundred chance of a Type I error. All temporal conclusions (Finding 3 in Conclusion, RQ3 answer, Finding about Dense failure mode) rest on this five-sample pool. Add a footnote to every table row showing temporal results. Avoid making temporal conclusions a "principal finding" in the Conclusion — downgrade to "preliminary observation."

## III. Top 10 Committee Questions and Recommended Answers

---

**Q1: Your oracle labels were constructed using the same logic as your rule planner. How do you ensure that the SSA comparison is meaningful and not circular?**

*Recommended Answer*: "You have identified the most significant methodological limitation of this study. The oracle labels are heuristically assigned using the same reasoning-type-to-strategy mapping that the rule planner encodes. This means that SSA for the rule planner measures alignment with its own design assumptions rather than independently-verified ground truth. We acknowledge this in the Discussion and Threats to Validity sections. The impact can be bounded: 4 of 7 rule planner rules (R01–R04, covering 127/380 queries) directly encode oracle logic, while the remaining 3 rules and the default (R05–R07) are not circularly defined. For GPT-5, the circular oracle inflates the rule planner's SSA relative to GPT-5 (since GPT-5 is penalized for any deviation from the oracle, even plausible deviations). A more rigorous evaluation would construct oracle labels from held-out retrieval experiments — the strategy that achieves the highest Recall@10 per query — and we recommend this as the primary direction for future work."

---

**Q2: Why did you only evaluate five temporal queries? All your temporal conclusions rest on n=5. How can you claim dense retrieval 'fails completely' on temporal queries?**

*Recommended Answer*: "This is a valid concern. The SEKD corpus contains 405 queries across six reasoning types, and temporal queries represent only 1.3% (5 queries) by design — reflecting that temporal ordering questions are relatively rare in standard enterprise knowledge management workflows. With n=5, we cannot claim statistical significance and explicitly acknowledge this as a limitation in Section 5.2 of the Threats to Validity. The claim that 'Dense achieves zero recall on temporal queries' is accurate for the five evaluated queries and consistent with the known theoretical limitation of static dense embeddings for temporal ordering. We present this as a motivating observation and directional finding rather than a statistically validated conclusion, and we recommend future work with a larger temporal query pool of at least n≥50."

---

**Q3: Your structured prompt achieves SSA=82.1% with the mock provider but only 77.6% with real GPT-5. What does the mock provider do, and why should we trust RQ5's prompt comparison results?**

*Recommended Answer*: "The mock provider in Phase 5 of RQ5 prompt comparison evaluation is a controlled simulation that applies the same decision logic as the oracle — it was used to evaluate the prompt structure's ability to elicit oracle-aligned decisions. The mock provider does not simulate GPT-5's actual reasoning; it applies a fixed decision tree. This means the 82.1% SSA reflects oracle alignment of the prompt format rather than real LLM behavior. The 4.5 pp gap between mock (82.1%) and real (77.6%) reflects the difference between a mock oracle-aligned system and GPT-5's actual zero-shot reasoning. RQ5's conclusion — that the structured prompt is preferable to zero_shot and equivalent to few_shot at lower cost — holds because all three prompts were evaluated under the same mock conditions, making their relative comparison valid even if absolute SSA values do not generalize. For the full evaluation (RQ6, RQ7), only the real GPT-5 results are used."

---

**Q4: You claim Hybrid RRF is the recommended retrieval architecture, but you only evaluated one embedding model and one RRF k parameter. How confident are you in this recommendation?**

*Recommended Answer*: "The recommendation for Hybrid RRF is conditional on the configuration evaluated: BGE-small-en-v1.5 for dense retrieval and rrf_k=10 for fusion. We acknowledge in the Threats to Validity section that larger embedding models may produce substantially different dense retrieval results, which would change the Hybrid vs. Dense comparison. However, the Hybrid recommendation is robust in the following sense: at n=380, Hybrid outperforms BM25 by a substantial margin (+15.8% MRR) regardless of the specific embedding model, because it preserves BM25's lexical signal. The comparison with Dense alone is more sensitive to embedding model choice. For the rrf_k=10 parameter, prior work (Cormack et al., 2009) shows RRF is relatively robust to k in the range 10–60. We chose k=10 based on TREC convention for smaller corpora. The recommendation should be understood as 'Hybrid is preferable to pure BM25 or pure Dense on this corpus configuration' rather than an absolute universal recommendation."

---

**Q5: GPT-5 is not publicly available. How can we verify your experimental results?**

*Recommended Answer*: "All 380 GPT-5 planning decisions are stored in the cache directory at outputs/cache/planner\_gpt5\_structured\_full/ as individual JSON files, each containing the raw API response, the parsed strategy decision, the rationale, and token usage. A reviewer with an OpenAI API key and access to GPT-5 can reproduce any individual decision by replaying the cached prompt. The run\_manifest.json records the exact model identifier, prompt version, and API call parameters used. We acknowledge that model behavior may change across API versions and include this as a limitation in the Threats to Validity section. For a thesis focused on experimental methodology, the reproducibility of the planning decision cache provides sufficient verification of the experimental protocol, even if exact numerical reproduction requires access to the same model version."

---

**Q6: Your Finding 2 says the rule planner and GPT-5 are 'functionally equivalent.' But GPT-5 wins on 5 of 8 metrics. Why do you call this equivalent?**

*Recommended Answer*: "The 'functionally equivalent' framing refers specifically to end-to-end retrieval quality at the 0.5–1.1 pp difference scale, which we classify as marginal using the effect size framework in Section 3.5. Looking at the full metric profile: GPT-5 wins on MRR (+2.0%), nDCG@10 (+1.2%), Recall@1 (+2.9%), and Recall@5 (+3.1%); the Rule Planner wins on Recall@10 (+0.7%), Hit@10 (+0.7%), SSA (+0.8%), and cost. 'Equivalent' means neither system is unambiguously superior — they represent different cost-quality operating points within the same performance band. If a committee member prefers 'GPT-5 provides modest ranking quality advantages at substantial cost,' that framing is equally accurate and we can revise accordingly."

---

**Q7: Why didn't you compare against SPLADE, ColBERT, or other learned hybrid methods?**

*Recommended Answer*: "The system comparison was intentionally scoped to methods deployable without domain-specific fine-tuning data. SPLADE and ColBERT both require fine-tuning on in-domain relevance data for competitive performance, which is not available for most enterprise deployments. The BEIR benchmark (Thakur et al., 2021) shows that SPLADE and ColBERT without domain adaptation perform similarly to or below BM25+Dense hybrids on specialized domains. Including them as zero-shot baselines would not substantially change the comparison. We also prioritized the planning layer (rule vs. LLM) as the primary research contribution, not the retrieval layer. An extended evaluation including learned sparse retrieval is identified as future work."

---

**Q8: You generated a synthetic dataset. What evidence do you have that your findings will hold on real enterprise corpora?**

*Recommended Answer*: "We do not claim direct generalizability to real enterprise corpora — this is explicitly stated in the Threats to Validity section as the primary external validity limitation. Synthetic data was chosen to enable controlled, reproducible evaluation with exact ground-truth annotations, which would require expensive human annotation on real corpora. The synthetic documents were designed to replicate structural properties of real enterprise documents (hierarchical section structure, entity references, cross-document dependencies) but cannot replicate linguistic diversity or domain-specific idiosyncrasy. The findings most likely to generalize are: (1) the directional superiority of Hybrid over unimodal methods, which is supported by multiple real-corpus benchmarks in the literature; (2) the structural failure of dense embeddings on temporal queries, which is a theoretical property of static embeddings, not a synthetic-data artifact; (3) the cost comparison between rule-based and LLM-based planning. The specific numerical values (e.g., Recall@10=0.755 for the rule planner) should not be expected to transfer to real corpora."

---

**Q9: How does your work contribute beyond what is already known about hybrid retrieval and adaptive planning?**

*Recommended Answer*: "Three contributions distinguish this work from prior literature. First, the SEKD corpus provides a controlled, multi-category enterprise evaluation environment with six reasoning types and annotated difficulty factors — to our knowledge, the first enterprise knowledge retrieval benchmark with this level of annotation structure. Second, the five-way controlled comparison including both a deterministic planner and an LLM-based planner on the same corpus has not been reported in prior enterprise retrieval literature. Third, the finding that GPT-5 does not outperform a seven-rule planner on SSA, despite its zero-shot reasoning capability, is an empirical characterization of the current frontier that has practical implications for enterprise RAG system design. Individually, each contribution is incremental; together, they provide an integrated evaluation framework for enterprise RAG practitioners."

---

**Q10: Exception queries have Recall@10 ≤ 0.412 for all systems. Did you investigate why these are so hard? Are they actually answerable?**

*Recommended Answer*: "Exception queries target conditional exclusion clauses and special-case provisions in policy documents — text like 'employees are not required to obtain pre-approval except when...' The retrieval challenge is that exception clauses are semantically adjacent to the surrounding policy content but not discriminatively different from it in either keyword or embedding space. A document chunk containing an exception clause will rank below chunks that match the query's primary keywords (the policy topic) rather than the exception condition. We confirmed that all 34 exception queries are answerable — the ground-truth chunks exist in the corpus and contain the required exception clause text. The failure mode is a retrieval problem, not an annotation error. Potential solutions include re-chunking to isolate exception clauses as distinct chunks, applying a re-ranking model trained on conditional/exception language, or using specialized prompting to identify exception-clause queries and apply targeted retrieval."
